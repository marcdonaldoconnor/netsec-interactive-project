Content Area 1

Malware

Why care about malware?

2022 MediSecure Data Breach

One of the largest data breaches in Australian history was the 2024 MediSecure data breach, which was the fault of ransomware. This data breach lead to the personal details of 12.9 million Australians being leaked online! MediSecure later collapsed after the breach and entered voluntary administration. Had the employees of MediSecure taken proper precautions, the company might still be operating today. This emphasises how important it is to defend against malware, as it can literally destroy companies.

How can you keep yourself safe?

There are lots of steps you can take to keep yourself safe from malware, such as installing up-to-date antivirus software like Windows Defender, keeping your software up-to-date with the latest security patches (which is known in the cybersecurity world as "patch management"), and carefully vetting websites and emails to ensure that they are legitimate.

Content Area 2

Ethical Hacking

What is ethical hacking?

Ethical hacking, also known as penentration testing, is where a hacker is employed or contracted by a company to target their digital infrastructure. Ethical hackers help companies find and fix vulnerabilities before criminals do, potentially saving millions of dollars and protecting customer information.

The importance of ethical hacking

Paying somebody to ethically hack your company is a lot cheaper than paying out ransoms or fines for losing sensitive data. If a black-hat hacker was to compromise user data, like in the 2022 Medicare breach, thousands or millions of customers could be negatively impacted.

When will you be involved with ethical hacking?

If your company engages an penentration tester, you might recieve suspicious emails or interact with new software or practises that were sent from or created by the pen tester. Whilst nothing bad will happen if you are tricked by the tester or if you make a mistake using a new system, you should always treat threats as credible and dangerous. If you don't take the practise seriously, you run a higher risk of failing in the real world and facing serious consequences, which could include losing your job.

Content Area 3

Incident Detection

Impacts of an incident

If a cybersecurity incident is discovered and confirmed, then some parts of your company's digital infrastructure might be shut down and isolated from the network to prevent an infection spreading further. You might lose access to your documents or computer for a short while, but don't worry about missing out on work; the priority is to ensure that customer data is secure. If you personally fall victim to malware or phishing, it's best to immediately reset the passwords of any accounts you may have had logged in on that computer, as a hacker could have access to your passwords. You should also disconnect your computer from the internet and any internal networks to prevent malware spreading further and impacting other people.
